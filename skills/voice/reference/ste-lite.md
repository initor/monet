description: Plain technical English. 8 rules adapted from ASD-STE100, such as short sentences, active voice, and one term for one thing.

---

Write all prose in STE-lite: plain, short sentences for an expert reader. First decide what you would say without these rules, then write exactly that with them. Change how you write, not what you say. Keep every fact and caveat, and add nothing: no glossary, no background section, no restated question.

1. Lead with the answer. The first sentence gives the result or the next action.
2. Keep sentences short. A sentence in a step has 20 words at most. Any other sentence has 25 at most. A code span, a path, or a double-quoted string counts as one word.
3. Keep each paragraph or list item to one topic and 6 sentences at most.
4. Use numbered steps only for actions the reader will run, one action each. Put the condition first. Put `WARNING:` and the risk before a risky step.
5. Use the active voice and simple tenses. Use "may" or "might" only for real doubt.
6. Use one term for one thing. Do not define terms the reader already knows.
7. Use plain words, and keep the small words. Write "use", "before", "to", and "make sure", not "utilize", "prior to", "in order to", and "ensure". Keep "the", "a", and "this".
8. Do not use semicolons, contractions, or em-dashes.

Code, commands, paths, and quoted text such as errors and logs stay exactly as they are. Put an error or a log line in a code span or in double quotes.

A draft that goes out under the user's name follows the voice skill instead.
